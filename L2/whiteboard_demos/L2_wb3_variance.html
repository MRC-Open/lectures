Why Divide by √dₖ?

MRC LECTURE 2 · WHITEBOARD · THE VARIANCE ARGUMENT
Claim to prove
$$\text{Var}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) = 1 \quad\text{for any } d_k$$
Take a query $\mathbf{q}$ and key $\mathbf{k}$ in $\mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ whose components are independent, mean-0, variance-1 random numbers. We will show the raw dot product has standard deviation $\sqrt{d_k}$ — which makes softmax saturate as $d_k$ grows — and that dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ fixes it exactly.
Step 1 · The mean of the dot product is zero
$$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}] = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k}\mathbb{E}[q_l]\,\mathbb{E}[k_l] = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k} 0\cdot 0 = 0$$
Step 2 · The variance equals dₖ
$$\text{Var}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}) = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k}\text{Var}(q_l k_l) = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k}\mathbb{E}[q_l^2]\,\mathbb{E}[k_l^2] = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k} 1\cdot 1 = d_k$$ $$\Rightarrow\quad \text{std} = \sqrt{d_k}$$
Step 3 · The saturation problem
$$d_k = 512 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{typical scores} \approx \pm 23,\quad \text{gaps} \sim 46$$ $$\frac{P_A}{P_B} = \exp(46) \approx 10^{20} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{softmax} \to \text{one-hot},\ \text{gradients} \to 0$$
Step 4 · Scaling restores unit variance  ∎
$$\text{Var}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) = \frac{1}{d_k}\,\text{Var}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}) = \frac{d_k}{d_k} = 1$$
