Why Divide by √dₖ?

MRC LECTURE 2 · WHITEBOARD + PICTURE · THE VARIANCE ARGUMENT, MADE CONCRETE
Claim to prove
$$\text{Var}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) = 1 \;\text{ for any } d_k$$
Step 1 · the mean is zero
$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}] = \sum_l \mathbb{E}[q_l]\,\mathbb{E}[k_l] = 0$
a random query and key are, on average, orthogonal
Step 2 · the variance equals dₖ
$\text{Var}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}) = \sum_l 1 = d_k \;\Rightarrow\; \text{std}=\sqrt{d_k}$
$d_k$ independent ±1 terms — a random walk; spread grows as $\sqrt{d_k}$
Step 3 · so larger dₖ saturates softmax
$\text{std}=\sqrt{d_k}\uparrow \;\Rightarrow\; \text{scores}\uparrow \;\Rightarrow\; \text{softmax}\to\text{one-hot}$
the same Step-2 growth, fed into softmax: bigger dₖ → flatter gradient
Step 4 · scaling restores unit variance  ∎
$\text{Var}\!\left(\dfrac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) = \dfrac{d_k}{d_k} = 1$
scores back to order ±1, softmax stays graded, gradients stay alive
