Why This Course Exists

Four foundational concepts. Four fields. One equation.

This course is built on four concepts that are settled science — textbook material in their respective fields, none in dispute by working scientists in those fields.

  • Boltzmann's distribution (1877) — statistical mechanics
  • Maximum entropy principle (Shannon 1948, Jaynes 1957) — information theory
  • Softmax attention mechanism (Vaswani et al., 2017) — machine learning
  • Luce's choice axiom (1959) — choice theory / econometrics

These four pillars are mathematically the same equation — the convergence equation. The course teaches you to see the identity, then shows that the identity, combined with five physical conditions, prescribes a complete neural architecture for predicting molecular binding: the Specificity Foundation Model (SFM).

The Convergence Equation

Four independent derivations. One function.
1877 · Physics
Boltzmann
statistical mechanics
$P(\text{state}) \propto \exp(-E/k_BT)$ at thermal equilibrium
1948–57 · Information theory
Shannon · Jaynes
maximum entropy principle
Most-rational distribution under partial info → exponential form
1959 · Choice theory
Luce
independence of irrelevant alternatives
IIA + monotonicity force the softmax form (uniqueness theorem)
2017 · Machine learning
Vaswani et al.
transformer attention
$\mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top / \sqrt{d_k})\, V$
$$P(j) \;=\; \frac{\exp(s_j)}{\sum_k \exp(s_k)}$$
Four derivations, four fields, 140 years apart. Not analogies — an algebraic identity.

Why No Existing Training Prepares You

A structural gap, not a failure of anyone's preparation
  • Standard ML courses teach attention — but not its identity with Boltzmann
  • Standard biophysics teaches Boltzmann — but not its identity with attention
  • Standard information theory teaches max entropy — but not its prescriptive consequences for neural architectures
  • No existing curriculum stitches these together — because none was designed to. The implications of Level 3 alignment — where the architecture is the governing equation rather than approximating it — have to be learned explicitly.

The math used in this course is at the level of college algebra and basic calculus — dot products, exponential functions, derivatives. Detailed step-by-step derivations are provided in writing for every key result. Anyone in the lab can take this course. Wet-lab researchers, computational biologists, and immunologists will all start from the same place.

Final Project — Build Your Own SFM

With Claude Code · using CALM as the reference implementation

Each of you builds an SFM on your own molecular recognition data — antibody–antigen pairs from repertoire or display screening, TCR–pMHC pairs, peptide–MHC pairs, cytokine or other ligand binding to receptors, whatever fits your project.

  • You decide the domain-expert decisions: encoder selection, data curation, masking strategy, train/test split design and leakage prevention, hard-negative strategy, evaluation design
  • The architecture itself does not change — that part is prescribed by the convergence equation
  • You evaluate with retrieval metrics, out-of-distribution splits at sequence-identity-clustered thresholds, and pseudo-prospective validation against held-out experiments
  • You present your results to the lab. We then discuss the cross-domain comparison: what training behaviors emerge from your data, what they tell us about the framework, and what comes next.

This is the only test of understanding that matters: did you build a model that works on data nobody has seen before?

SFMs as Infrastructure

A method like PCR or CRISPR
Broad applicability
Any molecular recognition system at thermal equilibrium — antibodies, TCRs, MHC, TFs, miRNAs, CRISPR guides, enzymes, drug–target pairs. Six SFM domains demonstrated, four more in active development.
Low resource
CALM trained on ~4,000 antibody–antigen pairs vs. CLIP's ~400,000,000 image-text pairs. Single GPU node, pretrained encoders. Bottleneck is data curation by a domain expert.
Open source
CALM, the SFM template, training and evaluation pipelines, validation harness, pretrained encoders — all public. No proprietary infrastructure needed.
The natural division of labor
AlphaFold and the structure predictors solve structure. ESM-2, AntiBERTy, DNABERT-2, MoLFormer, RNA-FM solve sequence representation. Neither solves specificity — which partner does this molecule recognize from a pool of candidates? SFMs sit on top of this infrastructure and produce the specificity prediction itself.
Ten years from now: every recognition problem in biology has an SFM.
The model you build at the end of this course is the prototype.
