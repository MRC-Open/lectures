Proteins Don't Decompose by Position

Why a PWM-style table can't capture antibody–antigen binding
① DNA — what we just did
linear strand  ·  one number per (position, base)
−ΔG = Σp wp(basep)  a clean sum
Every position contacts the protein independently. Energy adds up. A table indexed by (position, base) captures the binding.
② Protein — what doesn't carry over
3D-folded chains  ·  ~20 contacts out of ~10⁴ possible residue pairs
−ΔG ≠ Σp wp(aap)  no position-by-position recipe
Which residues touch is set by the 3D fold, not by sequence order — and contacts are residue pairs, not single positions. The "position" axis a PWM needs simply isn't there.
③ And even at the contacts that ARE there, energies don't add — epistasis
a double-mutant example
Mutation A alone costs +1.0, mutation B alone +0.8.
Additive prediction: +1.8 kcal/mol.   Measured: +2.8.
ΔΔGAB ≠ ΔΔGA + ΔΔGB
One mutation changes the cost of the other — even if the contact list were known, no per-position table could reproduce this.
So for proteins the binding-energy landscape cannot be written as a sum over sequence positions  →  the decomposition still exists, but in a different basis. We have to learn it.
