Molecular Recognition Computing

A Tutorial Course for the Reddy Lab — Sai T. Reddy · ETH Zurich · Botnar Institute of Immune Engineering
Format  4 sessions · ~2 hours each · informal Dates  12 Jun · 17 Jun · 19 Jun · 1 Jul 2026 Assessment  Final project — build your own SFM

This course proves a single idea and follows it to its consequences: the softmax attention mechanism in modern AI is the same equation as the Boltzmann distribution that governs molecular binding. From that identity — plus five physical conditions any recognition system at thermal equilibrium satisfies — a complete neural architecture is prescribed: a Specificity Foundation Model (SFM). The goal is for each of you to learn enough to build one on your own data.

1Why This Course Exists

The course rests on four concepts that are settled science — textbook material in their fields, not new claims:

1877 · Physics
Boltzmann distribution
The probability of an energy state at thermal equilibrium ∝ $\exp(-E/k_BT)$.
1948–57 · Information theory
Maximum entropy
Shannon & Jaynes: the most-rational distribution under partial information is the exponential family.
1959 · Choice theory
Luce's choice axiom
Independence of irrelevant alternatives forces the softmax form — a uniqueness theorem.
2017 · Machine learning
Softmax attention
Vaswani et al.: the operational core of every transformer and language model.
$$P(j) \;=\; \frac{\exp(s_j)}{\sum_k \exp(s_k)}$$
The same function, derived independently in four fields. We call it the convergence equation.

The one genuinely new contribution — the only thing here that is not already textbook material — is the bridge: the convergence equation, combined with five physical conditions, prescribes a complete neural architecture for predicting binding. The proof is in Computational Convergence of Adaptive Immunity and Artificial Intelligence (Reddy, 2026); the architectural derivation in Methods for Molecular Recognition Computing (Reddy, 2026); the first SFM — CALM, for antibody–antigen specificity — in Lee et al. (2026).

2An Identity, Not an Analogy

Synthetic biology is full of useful analogies — genetic circuits are like electronic circuits; neural networks are inspired by neurons. What is claimed here is different. The convergence equation is not analogous to the Boltzmann distribution; under the appropriate identification of variables, it is the Boltzmann distribution. To our knowledge there is no other case in the history of computer science where a computational mechanism has been shown to be a literal mathematical identity with a physical one.

3Why No Existing Training Prepares You

This is structural, not a gap in anyone's background. ML courses teach attention but not its identity with Boltzmann; biophysics courses teach Boltzmann but not its identity with attention; information-theory courses teach maximum entropy but not its consequences for neural architectures. No curriculum stitches these together — so the implications of Level 3 alignment (where the architecture is the governing equation, not an approximation of it) have to be taught explicitly. The math used here is at the level of college algebra and basic calculus — dot products, exponentials, derivatives. Anyone in the lab can take this course; wet-lab, computational, and hybrid researchers all start from the same place. Detailed written derivation guides are provided for every key result, and the most important ones we work through together in class.

4What You Will Learn

1
The mathematical foundations of modern AI. Linear algebra at the level needed to read transformer papers; the transformer architecture; contrastive learning and the InfoNCE loss. Enough to read the relevant papers and understand them.
2
The physics that governs molecular binding. The Boltzmann distribution and binding probability; the dissociation constant and free energy ($\Delta G = RT\ln K_d$); why competitive binding is softmax; given vs. emergent bilinearity.
3
A new paradigm — physics-derived AI architectures. Luce's proof; the SFM architecture derived from physics; parameter estimation in a known equation vs. function approximation — and the measurably different training behaviour it predicts (exponential data scaling, fast convergence, OOD robustness).
4
The ability to build, validate, and implement your own SFM. The final project: build an SFM on your own molecular-recognition data with Claude Code and the CALM codebase. You make the domain-expert decisions; the architecture is prescribed.

Build, Validate & Publish Your SFM
Each of you builds an SFM on your own molecular-recognition data — antibody–antigen, TCR–pMHC, peptide–MHC, cytokine–receptor, transcription factor–DNA, drug–target, microRNA–mRNA, CRISPR off-target, enzyme–substrate, whatever fits your project — using Claude Code with the mrc-sfm-template as your starting scaffold. 6Course Infrastructure — MRC-Open

Your final project is built on dedicated open-source infrastructure designed so the work outlives the course. The infrastructure does three things at once: it gives you a clean, paper-grade starting point; it archives every finished SFM as a permanent, citable artifact; and it accumulates across cohorts so the field grows from year to year.

The big idea
A community space for Specificity Foundation Models. MRC-Open is the grand open-source organization for all MRC programs — built so today's tutorial is the first of many. Your work is hosted in the program node MRC-ETHZ-BIIE, a sibling to future programs at other institutions, all federated under MRC-Open.

From template to publication — the pipeline

1 · Start
Use the template
Click "Use this template" on MRC-Open/mrc-sfm-template. You get your own independent repo — fresh-minimal scaffold with the architecture, the evaluation, and a CLAUDE.md that tells the AI coding agent the architecture rules.
2 · Build
Build with Claude Code
You direct; the agent codes. Adapt the five domain-specific things (encoders ×2, data, pooling, OOD splits). Standardized pool-512 retrieval evaluation + MMseqs2 OOD splits at 40/60/80 % identity. Orthogonal-verifier audit (AlphaFold-3 / Boltz-2) of every numerical claim.
3 · Publish
Archive & publish
Submit to MRC-ETHZ-BIIE/<domain>SFM-<you>-<cohort>. Tag a release → Zenodo mints a DOI. Weights go to Hugging Face. Your entry joins the MRC-Open registry. Eligible projects proceed to preprint, paper, and/or invention disclosure.

Your final project is a real publication

Every finished SFM has the same shape and same rigor as dtSFM and GenLoop — those papers are what a strong final project looks like. The infrastructure makes that possibility concrete:

Citable artifactA permanent DOI via the GitHub → Zenodo integration. Cite your own SFM the way you cite any other paper.
Public modelHugging Face weights in MRC-ETHZ-BIIE. Anyone can download and use your SFM.
Preprint & paper potentialProject report written in methods-paper format. The strongest projects become preprints (bioRxiv) and, with further work, full publications.
Invention disclosurePatent-eligible if your SFM, generative loop, or downstream design is genuinely novel. We'll evaluate together and route via the ETH technology transfer office.
DiscoverabilityStandardized GitHub topics + the MRC-Open registry mean your SFM is findable among many SFMs by domain, author, cohort, or headline metric.
Cumulative researchFuture cohorts build on yours. Your repo and DOI become reference material; next year someone may extend it with a decoder, a generative loop, or a new evaluation.

The infrastructure is built around two simple principles. Paper-grade by default: standardized evaluation, OOD splits at all thresholds, an orthogonal-verifier audit of every numerical claim, and a structured report — the same standard applied to the lab's own published SFMs. Cumulative by design: every cohort's SFMs join the same archive, so 2027's students see 2026's work as reference material and build on it. The course is not a re-run; it is a growing body of research.

7Why SFMs Become a Method Like PCR or CRISPR

Broad applicability: the framework prescribes an SFM for any molecular recognition system at thermal equilibrium — antibodies, TCRs, MHC, transcription factors, microRNAs, CRISPR guides, enzymes, drug–target pairs. Low resource: SFMs train on tens of thousands of pairs on a single GPU node using pretrained encoders — CALM achieved its results with ~4,000 pairs where CLIP needed ~400,000,000. The bottleneck is data curation by a domain expert, not compute. Open source: CALM, the SFM template, the pipelines, and the pretrained encoders are all public. PCR and CRISPR became foundational because they were broadly applicable, low-resource, and open — and unlike them, building an SFM needs no wet-lab step, only a domain expert who understands their own data.

8SFMs as Infrastructure

AlphaFold solved structure prediction. ESM and the protein language models solved sequence representation. Neither solves specificity — given a molecule and a pool of candidates, which partner does it actually recognize? Specificity is the central question of immunology, drug discovery, and synthetic biology, and it is what SFMs are built to answer. The natural division of labour: structure predictors provide structure, language models provide sequence representations, and SFMs sit on top and produce the specificity prediction itself. AlphaFold and ESM are tools you use; SFMs are the framework you build in.

Ten years from now, every recognition problem in biology has an SFM — for antibody and TCR repertoires, kinase families, viral escape. Each lab builds and maintains the SFMs it needs, on the data it has. The model you build at the end of this course is the prototype of what will be used across academia, biotech, and pharma in the coming years.
