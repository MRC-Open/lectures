Why $S = QK^\top$ Gives Every Pairwise Score

MRC LECTURE 2 · WHITEBOARD + MATRIX · THE ALGEBRA ON THE LEFT, THE PICTURE ON THE RIGHT
Claim
$$S_{ij} = \mathbf{q}_i \cdot \mathbf{k}_j$$
Step 1 · definition of matrix multiplication
$S_{ij} = (QK^\top)_{ij} = \sum_{l} Q_{il}\,(K^\top)_{lj}$
row $i$ of $Q$, against column $j$ of $K^\top$
Step 2 · the transpose only swaps indices
$(K^\top)_{lj} = K_{jl}$
column $j$ of $K^\top$ is just row $j$ of $K$ — antigen $j$'s own vector
Step 3 · substitute
$S_{ij} = \sum_{l} Q_{il}\,K_{jl}$
Step 4 · that sum is a dot product  ∎
$S_{ij} = \mathbf{q}_i \cdot \mathbf{k}_j$
Rows are indexed by $i$ (antibodies), columns by $j$ (antigens). The outlined cell is $i = 1$ (Ab₁), $j = 3$ (Ag₃).
