Hard Negatives

Today’s SFMs train on in-batch negatives — every antibody paired with a random other antigen. Most are easy: wrong shape, wrong everything, trivially pushed away. The model rarely sees a hard negative — a molecule that looks right (same shape) but doesn’t bind (wrong charge). Mining those could sharpen the decision boundary where it matters.

Biological precedent: thymic negative selection — T cells are trained against self-antigens, the ultimate curated hard-negative set.

your SFM project could explore thisMine hard negatives for your domain (e.g. near-miss binders from a docking screen), add them to the batch, and measure whether retrieval and specificity improve.
