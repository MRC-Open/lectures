The Boltzmann Distribution

One equation — read four ways
P(j) = e−Ej/kBT Z = softmax(−E / kBT)
The single object we built across the last four slides — free energy → the landscape → filling the wells → why the exponential. Every symbol now has a job.
e−Ej/kBT — the Boltzmann factor
One antigen's statistical weight. A deeper binding well (more negative Ej) is exponentially more occupied — at body temperature, every extra ~0.6 kcal/mol roughly doubles the odds.
kBT — the temperature
The contrast knob. Cold → the tightest binder wins outright (argmax). Hot → every antigen looks equal (uniform). At 310 K, kBT = 0.62 kcal/mol.
Z = Σk e−Ek/kBT — the partition function
The denominator. It sums the weights of every competing antigen, turning raw weights into probabilities. Every thermodynamic quantity can be recovered from Z alone.
the whole form = softmax
Maximum entropy forced this exact shape — no other function survives the counting argument. It is the identical function a transformer computes over its scores. Same equation, different field.
maximum entropy → Boltzmann distribution → softmax
one function, derived independently in physics, information theory, and machine learning — no known exception at equilibrium in 150 years
